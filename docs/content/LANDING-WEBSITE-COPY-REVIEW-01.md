## 1. Executive Summary

The draft is directionally strong and unusually disciplined about evidence boundaries. It correctly avoids live-commerce claims, compliance promises, authenticity guarantees, and final pricing. The strongest work is the Passport framing: "context, not proof" is clear, cautious, and publication-minded.

The main issue is not positioning. It is editorial weight. The draft often reads like a publication governance memo embedded inside website copy. That caution is valuable for review, but public pages need fewer internal caveats, more direct visitor value, and cleaner CTA routing. The copy should be tightened before implementation so validation markers remain in the production process, not in the visitor experience.

The repeated validation markers should be consolidated into one owner-oriented register. Many items are duplicates or near-duplicates across inline sections, page-level marker lists, and the global queue. The deduplicated register below keeps only items that materially affect website publication.

Final recommendation: Ready with edits.

## 5. Tone Of Voice Review

The intended voice is careful, knowledgeable, transparent, and pre-launch honest. That is the right voice for a wine passport product with trust implications.

Tone strengths:

- Clear evidence discipline.
- Respectful of both consumers and wineries.
- Avoids hype about authenticity, compliance, and commercial results.
- Uses accessible consumer language instead of expert-only wine vocabulary.

Tone risks:

- Too many phrases sound like internal instructions: "the public website should," "copy must not," "the safest public language is."
- Repetition of "controlled," "reviewed," "valid public data exists," and "pre-launch" creates a defensive rhythm.
- Some benefit language is vague: "future collaboration," "public information layer," "prepared passport data."
- Winery copy can feel more like risk containment than commercial invitation.

Language to improve:

| Current pattern | Issue | Editorial direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "being built as" | Sounds tentative when repeated. | Use sparingly; pair once with a clear pre-launch notice. |
| "controlled conversation" | Internal and slightly cold. | Use "reviewed inquiry" or another approved phrase consistently. |
| "public information layer" | Abstract. | Tie it to bottle, winery, wine, and vintage records. |
| "where valid data exists" | Necessary but repetitive. | Use once per page, then rely on concise limitations language. |
| "should not present" / "must not claim" | Internal instruction. | Keep in review notes, not public body copy. |
| "prepared passport data" | Accurate but stiff. | Clarify the visitor benefit before the operational phrase. |

AI-sounding or generic phrases to watch:

- "sits at the meeting point"
- "controlled partner conversations"
- "shared public information layer"
- "future collaboration"
- "support learning"
- "make that relationship clear"

These are not fatal, but they should be reduced in final public copy.

## 8. Recommended Edits Without Changing The Original Copy

Recommended global edits:

1. Move validation markers out of public body copy before implementation.
2. Use one approved pre-launch notice pattern across pages.
3. Use one approved Passport limitations pattern across pages.
4. Reduce repeated phrases: "valid public data exists," "controlled," "reviewed," "being prepared," and "must not claim."
5. Convert internal editorial instructions into final public prose during the next copy pass.
6. Standardize CTA capitalization and destination mapping.
7. Keep "Passport" as the public concept label unless product naming changes.
8. Keep "blind tasting" until the naming decision is resolved.
9. Avoid expanding into `/partners` copy inside these four pages.
10. Use proof modules only after screenshots, sample records, or an asset-free launch decision are approved.

Recommended page edits:

| Page | Edit priority | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `/` | High | Finalize the first-screen positioning sentence and reduce the proof/status sections into shorter public modules. |
| `/wine-lovers` | High | Keep the emotional promise, but tighten caveats and make the waitlist action concrete. |
| `/for-wineries` | High | Lead with practical winery value, then place pilot/compliance boundaries after the proposition. |
| `/passport` | Medium | Preserve the careful claim boundary, but consolidate hierarchy, scan, and limitation explanations to reduce repetition. |

Suggested wording direction, not replacement copy:

- Prefer direct visitor value over internal policy framing.
- Prefer "recorded public bottle context" over abstract "public information layer" when describing Passport.
- Prefer one concise no-commerce statement per page instead of listing every unavailable transaction type repeatedly.
- Prefer role-based CTA labels that reveal destination and audience.
- Prefer cautious but confident verbs: "shows," "records," "connects," "helps explain." Avoid "proves," "certifies," "guarantees," and "ensures."
