### Editorial Decisions

1. Used "Passport" as the public overview label and kept "CAP Passport", "Bottle Identity", "EU QR", and "EU e-label" conceptually separate.
2. Positioned the homepage as an audience router, not as a catalogue of every product concept.
3. Used pre-launch language throughout and avoided live commerce, live booking, active subscriptions, purchases, payments, and final pricing.
4. Used "blind tasting" as the consumer-facing phrase until the "Blind Detective" naming decision is approved.
5. Treated consumer tasting notes and taste profile as planned learning tools, not as live storage, recommendation, or provenance features.
6. Described Passport as recorded public context and verification status, not as authentication, certification, compliance, or anti-counterfeit protection.
7. Kept winery copy business-focused while avoiding guaranteed commercial outcomes.
8. Routed partner interest to `/partners` only as a path, without writing partner page copy.
9. Used explicit editorial markers rather than softening missing facts into vague marketing language.
10. Kept legal, privacy, and terms as support links rather than primary marketing pages.
